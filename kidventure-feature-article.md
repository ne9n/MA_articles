## Introduction: Flight at the Fingertips

Every summer during EAA AirVenture in Oshkosh, Wisconsin, one corner of Pioneer Airport delivers an experience no flight simulator can match: commanding a real airplane in flight with your own hands.

At **KidVenture**, the **Control Line (CL) Flight Circles** give young attendees the tactile thrill of flight. Holding a control handle tethered to dual steel lines, a child feels the mechanical pull of lift, the power of an electric brushless motor, and the immediate response of the elevator. Over the past 30 years, this volunteer-run program has introduced more than **25,000 young pilots** to the basics of flight.

---

## The Roots of KidVenture Control Line

Control line flying at Oshkosh predates the formal establishment of KidVenture. Seeking engaging, hands-on flight activities for kids, **Sean Elliott**—a former Junior National Stunt Champion and future EAA Vice President—teamed up with **Jim Krueger** and the **Milwaukee Circle Masters** (AMA Charter #662).

The initiative expanded after a memorable recruiting mission. Elliott flew EAA's full-scale **Spirit of St. Louis** replica directly to the annual SIG Manufacturing control line contest in Montezuma, Iowa. Landing on the contest field, Elliott and Krueger approached competitor **Dan McEntee** to recruit experienced modelers from the St. Louis area, including **Bob Arata** and **Jan Potts**, to join the Oshkosh flight line crew. That handshake in Iowa ignited a multi-decade aeromodeling mission that has united clubs across the Midwest.

## Flight Operations at Pioneer Airport

Operating seven days during AirVenture week, KidVenture runs two circles for youth instruction located directly adjacent to the active grass runway at Pioneer Airport, sharing the field with vintage aircraft and Bell 47 helicopter operations. In addition, the team operates a third, larger circle dedicated to daily noon airshow demonstrations and after-hours flying.

Together, the training circles handle an average of **1,400 to 1,800 youth flights per week**, reaching record peaks of **2,500 flights** in a single convention. 

Operations rely on coordinated teamwork between the **Instructor Pilot** and the **Pit Crew**.

### The Ground Briefing
When a young flyer steps into the circle, registration volunteers welcome the family, fit the student with safety glasses, and introduce their instructor pilot. In the center of the 50-foot training circle, the instructor delivers a concise pre-flight briefing:
* **Elevator Operation:** Tilting the wrist backward pulls the top line, raising the elevator to climb; tilting forward lowers the elevator to descend.
* **Arm Extension & Line Tension:** Keeping the arm steady and walking in rhythm maintains the essential line tension needed to control the aircraft against gusting Oshkosh winds.

### Pit Crew & Hand Launch
At the perimeter of the circle, the pit crew prepares the trainer. Because operations take place on rough, hard-packed grass directly adjacent to Pioneer Airport's active grass runway, the airplanes omit landing gear for grass durability.
1. The pit crew member inspects the leadouts, connects the 3S LiPo battery, and arms the solid-state electronic timer.
2. With the motor spooling up to governed RPM, the pit crew holds the model level and delivers a smooth, steady hand launch along the circular flight path.

### The Dual-Handle Coaching Technique
As the airplane takes to the air, the instructor pilot stands side-by-side with the student. Using the **dual-handle coaching method** pioneered by the late Bob Arata and master instructors like Paul Barbour, the instructor places a reassuring hand directly over the student's on the handle.

First-time flyers naturally tend to over-control. The instructor's hand smooths out jerky movements and stabilizes level flight. The trainers are trimmed nose-heavy with desensitized control throws to keep the model forgiving. 

Together, instructor and student guide the airplane through gentle climbs, level flight, and shallow descents. The student gets the genuine physical feel of the aircraft through the lines. Eight to 10 laps—about 90 seconds of flight time—is ideal for a first-timer. 

After 90 seconds, the electronic timer ramps down motor power, and the aircraft settles onto the grass in a smooth belly skid. The student walks off the circle to enthusiastic cheers from their parents, proudly holding an official **KidVenture Pilot Certificate**.

## Airframe Evolution: From Balsa to the Eagle 1

Teaching thousands of beginners in hot, humid, and windy summer conditions demands near-indestructible equipment. The fleet evolved across five distinct eras:

1. **Club Member Balsa Models (Early 1990s):** Personal balsa trainers flown by founding club members using their own fuel, props, and equipment. Fragile balsa structures required constant field repairs.
2. **Cox Plastic Ready-to-Fly (Mid 1990s):** Commercial plastic trainers, such as the Cox PT-19 powered by .049 glow engines. They lacked power in gusty crosswinds, and molded plastic bodies cracked on rough grass arrivals.
3. **Bratco Skybabies & Norvel .061s (Late 1990s–2000s):** Standardized on Bratco Skybaby kits backed by SIG Manufacturing with dependable Norvel .061 Big Mig glow engines. While power was consistent, the relentless flight tempo caused two to three airframe casualties daily, requiring nightly rebuilds by master technician Art Johnson.
4. **The Coroplast "Tuff Baby" (2010–2018):** On the drive home to St. Louis, Bob Arata and Dan McEntee designed an unbreakable trainer built from 4mm fluted polypropylene (Coroplast yard signs) reinforced with wood spars. Drawn by Art Johnson, Bob Arata built five original *Tuff Babies* in 2010. Those five airframes flew thousands of flights over multiple seasons without retiring a single plane.
5. **The Modern Electric "Eagle 1" (Present):** Chairman Chris Sterner designed the current standard trainer, the Eagle 1. Built from 4mm Coroplast, it features a 30-1/4-inch wingspan, a 2807 brushless motor, and a dedicated solid-state electronic timer engineered by Dave Siegler. Electric power eliminated hot mufflers, glow fuel mess, starting batteries, and needle-valve adjustments, allowing instant battery turnarounds and quiet reliability.

*(Note: Complete construction details, full-size CAD plans, bill of materials, and Dave Siegler's electronic flight timer schematic for the Eagle 1 will be featured in an upcoming construction article in Model Aviation.)*

### Eagle 1 Specifications
* **Wingspan:** 30-1/4 inches
* **Wing Area:** 226 sq. in.
* **Airframe:** 4mm fluted polypropylene (Coroplast) with basswood spar
* **Flying Weight:** 14.5 oz. (with 3S 850mAh LiPo)
* **Power:** 2807 1300Kv brushless outrunner motor
* **ESC:** 20A–30A electronic speed control
* **Timer:** Custom Siegler solid-state electronic governor/timer
* **Propeller:** 7x4 or 8x4 electric composite
* **Lines:** 50-foot, .012-in. braided stainless steel lines
* **Flight Time:** 90 seconds (automatic governed ramp-down)
* **Landing:** Belly-skid onto grass (no landing gear to snag in turf)

## Notable Visitors & Industry Champions

The flight line at Pioneer Airport frequently attracts notable visitors from across aviation. Renowned aircraft designer **Burt Rutan** and EAA Vice President **Sean Elliott** are regular visitors, stopping by to observe instruction and support the next generation of modelers. 

The venue also draws international competitors, including French aerobatic champions **Gilbert and Véronique Béringer** (founders of Beringer Aero), who regularly visit the circles to coach youth and perform precision F2B aerobatic demonstrations.

---

## The Long-Term Impact

The true measure of KidVenture Control Line shows up years later:
* **Aviation Careers:** Former youth flyers frequently stop by Pioneer Airport wearing airline captain uniforms, military flight suits, or aerospace engineering credentials. They return to thank the team, recalling how holding that control handle at KidVenture sparked their career paths.
* **Scholarships:** Multiple alumni have gone on to earn private pilot licenses, EAA Young Eagles awards, and AMA college scholarships.
* **Generations Returning:** Adults who took their first flight in the 1990s now bring their own children and grandchildren back to the circle to experience the same thrill.

---

## Noon Airshow Demonstrations & Twilight Flying

In addition to youth instruction, the team takes to the larger demonstration circle during the daily noon airshow. When full-scale operations pause for lunch, master modelers demonstrate precision stunt, Carrier deck landings, Combat, and scale flying for the AirVenture crowds.

After youth instruction closes at 3:30 PM, the flight line shifts to after-hours sport flying. Once the afternoon airshow clears, instructors and guest flyers bring out pulse jets and sport models over Pioneer Airport. High-speed pulse jet flights draw big crowds with their loud roar and red-hot tailpipes against the dusk sky. Later, the crew gathers at Camp Scholler for shared meals, hangar talk, and the traditional Wednesday night volunteer barbecue.

## Help Wanted: Join the KidVenture Flight Line Crew

Launching up to 1,800 youth flights in a week requires a strong volunteer team. The Circle Masters Flying Club is actively expanding its flight line crew and seeking volunteers from across the aeromodeling community.

Whether you are an experienced control line flyer, an RC sport pilot, or simply an enthusiast who enjoys helping kids, we need your help on the flight line:

### Volunteer Roles Needed
* **Instructor Pilots:** Fly side-by-side with young beginners in the circle using the dual-handle technique. You do not need to be a contest champion—just a competent flyer with patience and enthusiasm for youth aviation.
* **Pit Crew & Technicians:** Handle rapid aircraft turnaround on the grass: swap 3S LiPo packs, inspect control leadouts, arm flight timers, and deliver smooth hand launches along the flight path.
* **Ground Crew & Registration:** Welcome families, fit students with safety glasses, coordinate the flight line queue behind safety ropes, log flights, and present official KidVenture Pilot Certificates.
* **Off-Season Airframe Builders & Equipment Donors:** Can’t attend AirVenture in person? You can help build the fleet from home! We need builders to construct 4mm Coroplast Eagle 1 airframes, assemble Dave Siegler timer circuits, or donate equipment (2807 brushless motors, 20A–30A ESCs, 3S 850–1300mAh LiPo packs, and .012-in. control line sets).

### Volunteer Benefits
Volunteers on the flight line receive:
* **Free EAA AirVenture Admission Wristbands** for scheduled volunteer days.
* **On-Site Parking Passes** on the convention grounds.
* **Free Volunteer Camping** at Camp Scholler in the reserved KidVenture compound.
* **Flexible 4-Hour Shifts:** Morning (8:30 AM to 12:30 PM) and afternoon (12:30 PM to 3:30 PM) shifts leave plenty of free time every day to explore the warbirds, watch the daily airshow, and browse the exhibit hangars.
* **Wednesday Night Volunteer Barbecue** and evening campfire camaraderie.

To volunteer or donate equipment, visit [circlemasters.com](https://circlemasters.com) or email `kidventurecl@outlook.com`.
